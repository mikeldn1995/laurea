2. Driving traffic to the online shop

For the service websites, I worked with AI to organise audience, service and location content, giving visitors relevant entry points and a clear route towards an enquiry. Alexander’s website, for example, separates the information for parents, young people and referrers, with a clear introductory-call action.

I reviewed Alexander’s Cloudflare overview for 19 September–19 October 2026. It reports approximately 1.67k Unique Visitors and 21.6k requests. The chart shows ongoing traffic with several spikes. This is evidence that the domain receives traffic, but it does not by itself establish which marketing activity caused those visits or whether traffic increased because of my changes.

Cloudflare’s overview identifies visitors from IP-based requests and can include bots and partial-content requests. I therefore treat these figures as traffic indicators, rather than verified human sessions or potential customers.

4. Analysing relevant conversions for various online businesses

I distinguish the action that creates value for each business. Brandit needs a verified credit purchase; a therapy practice initially needs a successfully received enquiry or introductory-call request. A page view, an email acknowledgement and a confirmed paying customer are different stages.

Alexander: four delivered emails represent two requests

I reviewed the Resend domain metrics for 5–19 October 2026 and checked the corresponding message types. There are four delivered emails: two introductory-call notifications to the practice and two acknowledgements to the visitors. The correct count is therefore two request notifications, rather than four leads. Delivery does not establish a confirmed appointment or paying client.

I would not divide those two requests by Cloudflare’s 30-day IP-based visitor figure: the periods and populations differ. A defensible conversion rate requires the same date range, human sessions, deduplication and the exclusion of tests. The small delivery sample also cannot establish a long-term delivery rate.

The application sends a notification to the therapist and an acknowledgement to the visitor. These previews show the actual project templates using one assessment scenario; they illustrate the workflow rather than the private enquiries counted above.

5. Supporting the customer’s buying process

Brandit: clarity before the action, feedback afterwards

The same assessment workspace below follows an invoice batch from composition to review and document preview. Brandit’s public site: from the offer to a discovery call

The live booking page explains that the introductory video call is free and lasts 30 minutes. Visitors choose a day and time, then enter their name and email. It explains the confirmation and call-link emails, and provides an email alternative if the times do not suit. This helps people understand the commitment and what happens next before confirming a booking.

Service websites: helping visitors make an informed next step

Alexander’s site explains the introductory call, lets visitors choose their route and provisional time, and offers a message alternative. Alicia’s site makes fees, response expectations and contact options visible. These are different business models, but both require clear information, manageable steps and feedback about what happens next.
